# Professor review and question-bank provenance

Review date: 13 October 2026 (Asia/Muscat).

## Result

`src/data/questions.json` contains **165 original practice MCQs**, 15 for each of the 11 supplied lectures. Each chapter has five recall questions, five mechanism questions and five application questions. Every item has four options, one correct answer, explanatory feedback that addresses the distractors, a topic, a difficulty label and a one-based source PDF page.

**No authenticated public National University of Science and Technology, Oman / Oman Medical College biochemistry examination paper was located.** The bank must therefore be presented as original lecture-based practice, never as university past questions, recalled exam questions, an official exam bank or a prediction of future examinations.

This review samples understanding across all 11 lectures. A score is a practice result, not proof of complete mastery or a validated educational assessment. The questions have not been psychometrically calibrated or independently signed off by a human faculty member.

## Past-paper investigation

The search used three angles: official university/course/library pages; Oman Medical College student-attributed documents; and exact-topic old examination searches spanning enzymes, carbohydrates, PDH/TCA/ETC, glycogen, lipids, heme and iron/B12/folate. Six Exa paper-discovery searches requested 50 result slots in total. A separate initial web search was used for exact-name reconnaissance. These are search-result slots, not 50 authenticated relevant papers. Later searches verified specific scientific corrections rather than finding exams.

Representative search phrases included:

### Official institutional findings

1. The official [Biochemistry department page](https://nu.edu.om/medicine/biochemistry/) identifies the MD2 Biochemistry and Genetics course as **MPRC0205**, describes its biochemical scope and names the teaching faculty. It is not an examination-paper repository.
2. The official [College of Medicine library page](https://nu.edu.om/medicine/library/) describes books, MCQ books, reference and interlibrary services. No public biochemistry past-paper archive was identified on that page.
3. The official [E-Resources page](https://nu.edu.om/medicine/e-resources/) lists services including AccessMedicine and USMLE Easy. These are learning resources rather than evidence of a publicly available Oman university exam archive. Some resources require institutional/subscription access.
4. The [Library FAQ](https://nu.edu.om/medicine/library-faq/) points users to the university library site for e-resources and to MyNU for book renewal. It does not establish whether private exam archives exist.
5. The [College homepage](https://nu.edu.om/medicine/) identifies SOLE as the learning/teaching/assessment portal. No authenticated account or private SOLE/MyNU content was accessed in this task.

### Similar-name false positives excluded

- `exampapers.nust.na` is Namibia, not Oman. Its BIO521S and other papers were excluded.
- A 2013 MBM1202 paper headed National University of Science and Technology, Faculty of Medicine appeared in third-party results without an authenticated Oman connection; it was excluded.
- National University of Malaysia, Bangladesh National University, National University of Loja, Indian university exams, UNE and Lusaka Apex Medical University results were excluded.
- Unverified commercial question sellers and unattributed Scribd compilations were not treated as official institutional evidence.

The outcome means **not found in this public investigation**, not that no old papers exist. University library/faculty or authorized student portal access may provide genuine papers, but no restricted access was bypassed and no staff or students were contacted.

## Academic corrections and qualifications

These findings explain why the presentation should not reproduce every source-slide statement verbatim. Source PDFs remain intact; teaching statements and practice answers use the corrected or qualified interpretation.

| Lecture / page | Issue | Treatment in teaching material |
|---|---|---|
| Enzyme classification p. 6 | Six traditional classes presented as complete modern classification | Preserve the traditional classes and add modern EC 7 translocases, supported by [IUBMB](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC7/). |
| Enzyme classification p. 20 | Trypsin list includes His | Canonical preference is cleavage after Arg/Lys, per the [IUBMB trypsin entry](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC3/4/21/4.html). |
| Enzyme kinetics pp. 42–45 | Km described as affinity without assumptions | Use apparent affinity cautiously; Km is a kinetic parameter and is not generally identical to a binding dissociation constant. |
| Inhibition pp. 8–9 | Unchanged Km attributed broadly to noncompetitive inhibition | Specify the **pure** noncompetitive case; mixed inhibition need not leave Km unchanged. |
| Inhibition p. 20 | Positive/negative effectors equated with positive/negative cooperativity | Distinguish heterotropic allosteric effectors from substrate cooperativity. |
| Inhibition pp. 11–13 | All mechanism-based recovery implicitly requires new enzyme | Qualify the allopurinol example: the reduced-enzyme/oxypurinol complex can reoxidize, dissociate and reactivate, supported by the [2023 primary JBC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511816/). |
| Glycolysis p. 3 | Aerobic description may imply glycolysis itself consumes oxygen | Glycolytic reactions are cytosolic and do not directly require oxygen; downstream pyruvate/cofactor fates determine aerobic oxidation. |
| Glycolysis p. 6 | Pancreatic GLUT2 model generalized to human β cells | Human β-cell glucose transport differs from the common rodent model, supported by [primary human/rat research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC185903/). |
| TCA p. 3 | Glycolysis enzymes incorrectly placed in matrix | Glycolysis is cytosolic; PDH is mitochondrial. The [NCBI-hosted cell biology chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26894/) describes mitochondrial import of glycolysis-derived pyruvate. |
| ETC pp. 11, 17 | Older 3/2 ratios shown alongside modern 2.5/1.5; uncoupling described as “NO ATP” | Use approximate 2.5/1.5 teaching ratios and distinguish decreased oxidative yield from absence of all cellular ATP. |
| Carbohydrates p. 22 | All polysaccharides called nonreducing without qualification | Their reducing activity per mass is small; a blanket claim of chemically no reducing end is too strong. |
| Lipids pp. 10–12 | Broad “good/bad” labels and uniform eicosanoid actions | Use molecular structure and mediator-specific effects; avoid implying that all prostaglandins or thromboxanes have identical actions. |
| Heme p. 5 | Cytosolic step 5 omitted in compartment range | State mitochondrial step 1, cytosolic steps 2–5, mitochondrial steps 6–8. |
| Iron/B12/folate p. 14 | “Limited[No] tissue storage” of folate | Stores are limited relative to B12 but not absent. [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/) describes total body stores of about 15–30 mg, approximately half in liver. |
| Iron/B12/folate p. 26 | B12 deficiency universally attributed to intrinsic-factor malabsorption | IF-related disease is one cause; [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/) covers dietary and other absorption-related risks. |
| Iron/B12/folate pp. 29–30 | Schilling test presented as routine and described using liver-region radioactivity | Keep historical context out of current diagnostic recommendations. [NICE NG239](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/recommendations) uses total/active B12 and selected metabolic/cause tests in contemporary pathways. |

### Presentation text review

I reviewed `src/data/chapters.js` for academic consistency. Its concise bullets and deeper notes generally preserve the distinction between schematic models and measured structure/data. Two factual edits were made directly: the allopurinol recovery qualification above, and replacement of “globin is recycled” with the more precise statement that globin is degraded to reusable amino acids. Primary/reference links for trypsin, human β-cell transport and oxypurinol reactivation were added to the source list.

The page associated with a teaching scene is an entry point into its lecture; some scenes synthesize multiple pages. The chapter reference identifies the broader relevant range. For the MCQs, the source page was selected for the specific tested concept. External additions should retain their source links in the presentation reference panel.

## Source limitations and instructional scope

- Amino acids, carbohydrate chemistry, glycolysis and TCA do not print a conventional bibliography on their last page. Their closing pages are closing slides, not reference lists. No bibliography was invented for them.
- The amino-acid outcomes mention listing all 20 amino acids and biologically important peptides, but the supplied lecture does not provide a detailed complete treatment of every such outcome. The presentation and bank should not claim that every listed learning outcome has received equal examination depth.
- The ETC reference slide prints Chapter 5 and pages 85–94; that bibliographic numbering should be checked with the instructor/actual edition rather than silently treated as verified textbook indexing.
- Clinical patterns in questions are teaching associations. A single laboratory result is not presented as a definitive real-patient diagnosis, and treatment dosing has not been made into practice questions.
- Numeric P/O values are explicit teaching assumptions. Kinetic calculations use the simple Michaelis–Menten model; the bank avoids pretending that every enzyme, especially cooperative/glucokinase behaviour, fits it exactly.
- Contact-sheet figures and OCR can miss fine print; native diagrams and textbook references should remain available for faculty verification. No claim is made that every decorative image is original, licensed for redistribution, or scientific evidence.
